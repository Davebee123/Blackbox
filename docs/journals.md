# Journals

**Status.** Drafted, not built. Facts from docs/lore.md; the storyline's beats from docs/story-script.md.

## How they surface (hand-off: lore.md 9.2, session 2)

- **A journal entry is a file in a vault**, one entry a file, on the floor its header names.
- **It lands on a Journals page in the Ledger.** Every entry is ??? until found, and none is timed.
- **The page is a timeline** from 2049 to 2091 with the Cascade marked (02:14, 10 November 2061). Each found entry
  sits at its date.
- **Dates.** *C−N* is the Nth year before the Cascade, *C+N* the Nth year after; the day itself is *C0*. So 14 March
  2058 is C−4, 2 May 2090 is C+29, and now, 2091, is C+30.
- **Found on.** A floor that existed when the entry was written (lore.md 1). Entries from before the Cascade come from
  Bedrock, or from a restored archive on a later floor.

## Margit Sorensen

**Margit Sorensen** · Night-desk operator · Kessler Systems · *22 June 2049 · C−13* · found on 8 Bedrock, Kessler's night-desk terminal

> Annick Life joined the clearing house tonight. Their engineers wheeled forty servers in on trolleys and racked them
> while we watched, and at one in the morning the machine took them in as if they had always been there. A process that
> had been running on rack two walked across to one of the new boxes and carried on. I watched it go on the monitor.
> The senior says that's the whole idea: one machine, however many boxes you give it.
>
> We settled nine hundred thousand trades between midnight and six. Most were wages and rent. The rest were banks
> paying each other for the day's mistakes. A trade comes in, both sides answer, and it closes in a second. If one side
> doesn't answer, the timeout takes it back and everyone tries again in the morning. Twelve of those tonight. I logged
> each one in the blue book, which nobody reads.
>
> Erik bought a test bench at the Kessler surplus sale and has put it on our kitchen table. He says he's going to build
> a radio. I eat my breakfast standing up.

**Margit Sorensen** · Night-desk operator · Kessler Systems · *3 March 2051 · C−11* · found on 8 Bedrock, Kessler's night-desk terminal

> The members voted for finality this afternoon. From tonight a payment that goes in stays in until the other side
> answers, however long that takes.
>
> The senior was on the call for an hour before the vote. I heard his half from the kettle. He had a printout of every
> trade the timeout had saved in ten years, and he read some of them out: a hospital's payroll, a ferry company that
> went under at four in the morning with half its fuel bought. Halcyon's man let him finish, then said a rolled-back
> payment was a failed payment, and Halcyon paid the claims on failed payments. The rest of the members agreed with
> Halcyon. Brandt & Vey abstained.
>

> young man. At 23:40 he typed it in himself and signed the log, and under his signature he wrote that his objection was
> on file. Then he made us all tea, which he never does.

## Corin Wade

**Corin Wade** · Senior underwriter · Halcyon Mutual · *8 November 2050 · C−12* · found on 7 The Sink, in a restored archive

> Paid out on the Calloway Shipping claim today. Their payment for a cargo of grain went into Kessler's clearing house
> at four in the morning, the buyer's bank was slow to answer, and the timeout rolled it back. By the time Calloway sent
> it again the grain had been sold to someone else. Two hundred and six thousand, plus costs.
>
> That's the ninth this year. Each one is a policy doing exactly what it says, and each one comes out of my book.
>
> I took it to the board with a chart. A payment that can come back out of the clearing house is a payment that can
> fail, and we insure payments that fail. Kessler calls the timeout a safety valve. My chart shows what the valve
> has cost us, year by year. The board liked it. Dorothea wants a proposal to the members by the spring.
>
> On my way down the lift stopped on nine. Facilities say it's a fault in the controller and they've raised a ticket.
> The doors opened on an empty corridor and closed again.

**Corin Wade** · Senior underwriter · Halcyon Mutual · *3 March 2051 · C−11* · found on 7 The Sink, in a restored archive

> Finality passed at four this afternoon. Every member voted for it but Brandt & Vey, which abstained, and which old
> Vey will regret at renewal.
>
> Kessler's engineer had an hour, and he used all of it. He read out trades the timeout had saved, one at a time, in a
> flat voice, like a man reading a manifest. I let him finish. Then I said what I'd said to our board, and the members
> did the arithmetic on their own premiums.
>
> He asked one question at the end. What happens to a trade whose other side is never coming back? Dorothea said that
> banks don't vanish, and that we would insure the ones that might.
>
> We had dinner at the Marchmont. I had the duck. Our income on settlement cover will fall by a third and our claims by
> nine-tenths, and Dorothea raised a glass to the engineer, which I thought was gracious of her.
>
> The lift went straight down to the lobby without stopping.

**Corin Wade** · Senior underwriter · Halcyon Mutual · *14 May 2056 · C−6* · found on 7 The Sink, in a restored archive

> Kessler sells us a page every night now. *INIT: process table at 41%.* Legal asked what the number meant and I told
> them: every trade still waiting on an answer that will never come. Finality protects the insured ones. The rest sit
> in the table, and the table has a size.
>
> I wrote the clause myself. *Losses arising from failure of the underlying system are excluded.* Dorothea moved it up
> the page so it sits above the signature line on the renewal, in the same size type as everything else. That is the
> decent way to do it.
>

>

> coffee. Forty-one on Monday. Forty-one on Wednesday. On Friday it said forty-two.
